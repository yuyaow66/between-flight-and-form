When expectations become a weight.

A Shape of Her
← Back

A Shape of Her asks how changing social expectations shape Chinese women’s identities and choices throughout their lives. Growing up can mean being told who you should be before you have had the chance to decide for yourself. The project follows a female figure from childhood into old age, looking at how the expectations around her change. I am interested in the moments when what she wants comes into conflict with what is expected of her, and how she finds room to make choices of her own.

In childhood, this pressure may begin with being praised for being quiet or easy to look after. During adolescence, appearance can start to affect how a girl is treated, even when she wants to be recognised for something else. Adult life can bring expectations about marriage and family that conflict with her plans for work. The time and responsibility involved in caring for others can make those decisions harder. As she grows older, she may find that her wishes receive less attention, even as her experience grows. These pressures take different forms across communities in China, shaped by circumstances including class, family relationships and where a woman lives.
